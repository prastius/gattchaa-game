# Gattchaa Town: Gameplay Guide

Gattchaa Town is a small online world for card collectors, built around the Gattchaa Oripa shop. You walk around a walled royal town with other players, chat, open your own shop, and head out of the gates to fight monsters, level up and collect gear, much like Ragnarok Online or TibiaME.

The game has three parts:

1. **Hanging out.** The town is a meeting place. Chat, sit by the fountain, visit the Gattchaa staff, and run your own shop in the Market Square: trade gear, ingots and monster cards for gold, and show off your real cards.
2. **Adventure.** Outside the walls, monsters wait in fields sorted by level. Level up, choose a job, find gear and monster cards, and upgrade your equipment.
3. **Player vs player.** Challenge anyone to a duel, climb the ranking in the Arena, or fight anyone (and be fought) in the Wildlands, the open PVP field north of town.

> **One rule about money.** Real cards are bought and paid for only on gattchaaoripa.com. In-game gold buys in-game things (potions, gear, upgrades, other players' loot) and never real cards.

## 1. Getting started

- **Play as a guest** with just a name, or **Sign in with Gattchaa** using your gattchaaoripa.com account. The sign-in happens on the Gattchaa site, so the game never sees your password.
- **Guests** can do everything except open a shop (they can still buy from one), but they start over on their next visit.
- **Signed-in players** keep their character, level, bag, gear and skills on any device. Only one window per account is allowed: opening a second one takes over from the first.
- **Your look.** Choose a body, hair, clothes and colours when you join. You can change them any time with the **Wardrobe** button. Gear you wear (helmets, armour, weapons…) shows on top of your look.
- New players appear on the main avenue, between the fountain plaza and the Market Square.

## 3. The town

Gattchaa Town is a walled royal town in the middle of an island, with gates to the west, east and south.

- **Gattchaa Palace** (north) is the Gattchaa store. Oripa machines stand at its doors, one per franchise. Inside are a machine gallery, the Battle Hall, and counters for pickups and the newest packs.
- **The palace forecourt** has the Ichiban Kuji house and the Card Grading house.
- **The fountain plaza** is where the main street crosses the grand avenue. Wandering townsfolk here share tips.
- **The Rooms pavilion** (west of the plaza) has tables showing live Sharing Pack and Packs Battle rooms.
- **The pack stalls** (east of the plaza) are four counters run by Gattchaa staff.
- **The Market Square** (by the south gate) is where players run their own shops.
- **The lakeside park** runs along the west wall, with a pond, benches and a campfire.
- **The Collectors' Café** is in the east quarter, with **the Arena** behind it: a ring of sand where players duel, with benches for the crowd.
- **The gates** lead to the monster fields: south to the South Meadow, east to Dusk Hollow, west to the Orc Woods. From the west and east roads, paths run north along the town walls to the Wildlands. Off the south road, a path runs east to the meadow's south-east point, where a ferry sails to the snowy island of Frostfell. And the east road runs on past the Pumpking to a boat for the autumn island of Ravenmoor.

The town is always safe: monsters never come in, and nobody can attack anyone inside the walls, except in a duel both players agreed to.

**Day and night** follow Batam time. At night the lamps, windows, campfire and shop signs glow and fireflies come out. By day, butterflies fly over the flowers. When someone pulls a big hit on Gattchaa, the whole town hears about it.

## 4. Player shops

Ragnarok-style vending. Signed-in players sit down in the Market Square and sell two kinds of things:

- **Things from their bag, for in-game gold:** gear (upgrades and cards stay in it), monster cards, ingots, potions, loot.
- **Their real cards,** the ones they've listed on the Gattchaa marketplace. These are only ever bought on gattchaaoripa.com, never with gold.

### Opening your shop

1. Walk into the **Market Square** and press **Open shop** (V).
2. Give your shop a name (or leave it empty for "your name's shop").
3. Under **Sell from your bag**, tap **Sell** next to up to **12** things. Set a price in gold for each, and for stacks how many to sell. If you ask less than Bram the Outfitter would pay, the form warns you.
4. The form tells you if your spot is good: not in front of staff, and not so close to another shop that the signs overlap.
5. Press **Open shop here**. Your character sits down on a rug with a sign over your head. The sign shows a coin when you sell only for gold, a card when you also have Gattchaa cards, and how many things are for sale.

You need at least one thing from your bag or one card listed on Gattchaa. Gear you're wearing isn't offered: take it off first.

### While it's open

- What you put in the shop **leaves your bag** until it sells.
- When something sells, **the gold goes straight into your purse** and the chat tells you who bought what. This works **even while you're away**: when you leave the game, a look-alike minds your shop for **24 hours**, and the next time you come back, the chat tells you what sold.
- To change prices or what's for sale, close the shop and open it again.
- **Closing:** press **Close shop**. Whatever didn't sell comes back to your bag. If your bag is full, it waits for you and arrives as soon as you make room.
- **Sold out:** when everything has sold (and you have no Gattchaa cards to show), the shop closes by itself.

### Buying

- Click a shop's sign (or browse it from Tina's list) to see what it sells, with prices in gold.
- Press **Buy** for one, or **All** for the whole stack. You must be in the Market Square, have the gold and have room in your bag.
- Upgraded gear and gear with cards keep them when you buy them.
- Real cards in the shop link to their listing on gattchaaoripa.com, where you buy and pay as usual.

### Finding things

Ask **Tina** in the Market Square:

- **Shops** lists every open shop, who runs it, whether they're in town, and what it sells. Tap one to browse it.
- **Find an item** lists everything for sale for gold in every shop, cheapest first. Type a name ("mithril", "saber", "slime card") to narrow it down, then **Visit** the shop.

Shops also show as orange dots on the minimap.

Goblins, orcs, the undead and the beastmen of the Wildlands, Frostfell and Ravenmoor walk like people, carry real weapons (war axes, maces, flails, tridents, spears, bows) and lunge at you when they strike.

### How fighting works

- **Click a monster** (or press Space) and your character walks up and attacks it about once a second, as long as it's in reach. Bows and staves attack from a distance.
- **Missing.** You land 95% of your hits against monsters at or below your level. Each level the monster is above you takes 4% off, down to 60%.
- **Critical hits.** 5% of hits are critical: they ignore defence and do 1.5× damage. Some cards and skills raise this chance.
- **Defence** softens hits but never blocks them completely. Defence equal to the attack halves a hit.
- **Skills** always hit and never miss.

### Rewards

- **EXP** is shared by everyone who hurt the monster, by how much damage each person did. So a party sharing a boss all get EXP.
- **Gold** goes to whoever did the most damage.
- **Easy monsters give less.** A monster more than 5 levels below you gives 15% less EXP for each extra level, down to 10%.
- **Level-ups** heal you fully. Every 10th level is announced in chat.
- The level cap is **60** for now and will be raised step by step.

### Healing and fainting

- Out of combat you slowly get HP and MP back: a full heal takes about a minute.
- **Sit down** (X) to heal 3× faster. Resting **inside the town walls** doubles that again.
- **Potions** heal straight away, even in the middle of a fight: one every 0.8 seconds. Q drinks the smallest potion that heals you fully.
- **Fainting.** At 0 HP you faint and wake up back in town, fully healed. Novices lose nothing. Knights, Mages and Archers lose 3% of a level's EXP.
- **Beaten by another player** in the Wildlands, you lose nothing, unless you're an Outlaw (see "Open PVP: the Wildlands").

### Field bosses

**The Pumpking** lives where the east road ends in Dusk Hollow. It's a Ragnarok-style MVP:

- There's only one. It has 12,000 HP, breathes fire from a distance, and heals slowly if left alone.
- It's drawn big, with its name and health bar always showing.
- When it falls, the whole town hears who brought it down. It comes back **10 minutes** later, and the town hears that too.
- A level 20 Knight can just about beat it alone with plenty of potions. A party of three takes about 80 seconds.
- **It always drops** King's Candy (sells for 600 gold), a Mithril Ingot and usually an Orange Potion. It often drops good gear (Crusader Shield, Greathelm, Great Bow, Ember Staff, Knight's Blade), and half the time a Star Crystal. Rarely it drops the **Moonblade** (8%) or the legendary **Pumpking Card** (2%).

**The Orc Warlord** keeps his camp where the west road ends in the Orc Woods, among barrels, crates and a campfire:

- He has 24,000 HP, swings a halberd that hits very hard, and heals slowly if left alone. He's drawn big, in gold plate and a red cape.
- When he falls, the whole town hears who brought him down. He comes back **15 minutes** later.
- He's meant for a party: three or four players around level 28–30.
- **He always drops** the Warlord's Banner (sells for 1,500 gold), a Mithril Ingot and a Star Crystal (often two). He often drops good gear (Halberd, Viking Helm, Spartan Shield, Crossbow, Diamond Staff, Katana). Rarely he drops the **Warlord's Medal** (6%), and very rarely the legendary **Dragon Spear** or **Orc Warlord Card** (2% each).

**The Minotaur King** rules the Wildlands from his lair on the highest ground: four old stones round a fire.

- He has 45,000 HP, swings a huge war axe, and heals slowly if left alone. He's drawn very big, horned, in gold plate and a dark red cape.
- When he falls, the whole town hears who brought him down. He comes back **20 minutes** later.
- He lives in the Wildlands, so **other players can attack you while you fight him**. He's meant for a party of four around level 40: four level-42 players beat him in under two minutes, with one of them taking the hits and drinking potions.
- **He always drops** the Minotaur's Crown (sells for 3,000 gold), a Mithril Ingot and a Star Crystal (often two), and usually a White Potion. He often drops good gear (Trident, Close Helm, Engrailed Shield, Golden Plate, Flame Blade). Rarely he drops the **Minotaur Horn** (6%), and very rarely the legendary **Minotaur King Card** (2%), **Sunfire Bow** or **Archmage Staff** (1.5% each).

**The Frost Giant** keeps a ring of six old stones on Frostfell's icy highlands, where the path from the pier ends.

- He has 70,000 HP, swings a huge war axe, throws ice at anyone out of his reach, and heals slowly if left alone. He's drawn bigger than any other boss: blue-skinned, in silver plate, a horned helmet and a white cape.
- When he falls, the whole town hears who brought him down. He comes back **25 minutes** later.
- He's meant for a party of four around level 50: four level-50 players beat him in a few minutes, with one of them taking the hits and drinking potions.
- **He always drops** the Heart of Winter (sells for 4,500 gold), a Mithril Ingot and a Star Crystal (often two), and usually a White Potion. He often drops good gear (Glacier Lance, Glacier Bow, Aurora Staff, Valkyrie Helm, Frost Plate). Rarely he drops the **Frost Giant's Tooth** (6%), and very rarely the legendary **Frost Giant Card** (2%) or **Frost Scythe** (1.5%).

**The Fallen King** sits on his throne in the ruined castle on Ravenmoor's high ground, among broken walls and two rows of old pillars. Dark Knights still keep watch below his gate.

- He has 110,000 HP, swings a halberd, casts dark bolts at anyone out of his reach, and heals slowly if left alone. He's drawn very big: a crowned skeleton in gold plate and a purple cape.
- When he falls, the whole town hears who brought him down. He comes back **30 minutes** later.
- He's meant for a party of four at level 60: four level-60 players beat him in a few minutes, with one of them taking the hits and drinking potions.
- **He always drops** the Royal Key (sells for 6,000 gold), a Mithril Ingot and a Star Crystal (often two), and usually a White Potion. He often drops good gear (Dark Lance, Royal Bow, Soul Staff, Dread Plate, Ruin Shield). Rarely he drops the **King's Seal** (6%), and very rarely the legendary **Fallen King Card** (2%) or **Fallen Crown** (1.5%), a crown anyone can wear.

### Second jobs

At **level 50**, go back to **Orin** and take your **second job**. Each job has one, and it's for life too:

| First job | Second job | What changes | Lv 60 HP / MP / ATK / DEF |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Knight | **Lord Knight** | Pierces armour, sweeps crowds aside, and fights harder the longer it lasts. +69 HP, +10 ATK at the change | 1,083 / 208 / 154 / 50 |
| Mage | **Wizard** | Commands the sky: meteors, blizzards and lightning on whole crowds. +35 HP, +12 ATK at the change | 606 / 547 / 168 / 20 |
| Archer | **Hunter** | A falcon on the arm and snares in the pack: pins the prey down, then never misses. +45 HP, +10 ATK at the change | 746 / 281 / 153 / 30 |

- **You keep everything:** your level, EXP, gear, skills and skill points. A Lord Knight still wears Knight gear, a Wizard staves, a Hunter bows.
- **Three new skills** join your first three, on keys **4, 5 and 6**.
- Your stats grow a little faster from then on, so the change itself is a step up (you're fully healed too).
- There's no gift weapon this time, and the whole town hears about it.

### Skill points

- You get **1 skill point** at the job change, and **1 more every level** after that: 41 at level 50, 51 at level 60. That's enough to max five skills, but not all six, so choose.
- Spend them in the skills window (**K**). Each skill goes up to **level 10** and gets stronger every level.
- Use skills with keys **1–6** or the skill buttons. Each skill costs MP and has a cooldown, shown as a shadow on its button. There's also a short pause (0.7 s) between any two skills.
- Aimed skills use your target. Without one, they pick the nearest monster and walk you into range.
- Attack skills don't work inside the town.

## 9. The Blacksmith: upgrades and auras

**Hilda the Blacksmith** works by her forge fire outside the south gate. She makes your gear stronger, from **+0 to +10**.

### How upgrading works

- Every try costs **gold** and **one ingot**.
- **+1 to +4 always work.** After that, a try can fail.
- **A failed try drops the gear one level** (+6 fails and becomes +4). **It never breaks**, and you never lose the item.
- Upgrading works on weapons, shields, helmets, armour, legs and boots. **Rings and amulets can't be upgraded.**

## 10. Duels and the Arena

Fight other players, just for fun or for the ranking. **Nobody ever loses EXP, gold or items in a duel**, and both players are healed when it ends.

### How a duel works

1. **Challenge.** Click (or tap) another player standing near you and press **Challenge to a duel**. They get a pop-up and have 20 seconds to **Accept** or **Decline**.
2. **Countdown.** "3, 2, 1, FIGHT!" appears over both of you. You automatically aim at your opponent.
3. **Fight.** Attack like you would a monster: click them or press Space, and use your skills (keys 1–6). A bar across the top shows who you're fighting and the time left, with a **Give up** button.
4. **The end.** Whoever drops to 0 HP loses. The winner gets a "WINNER!" banner, and you're both healed straight away.

### Rules

| Rule | Why |
| --- | --- |
| Player hits do **half damage** | So a duel lasts long enough for skills and tactics to matter |
| **No potions** and no healing over time | Otherwise nobody could ever win. A Mage's Heal skill still works |
| **2 minutes** at most | After that it's a draw |
| Stay close: walking too far from your opponent calls it off | It's a draw |
| Giving up, leaving the game, or fainting to a monster | The other player wins |
| Duels work anywhere, even inside the town | Only the two duelists can hurt each other; nobody else can join in |
| You can't duel while minding your shop | Close the shop first |

Skills work on players too: aimed skills (Bash, Double Shot, Fire Bolt, Spiral Pierce, Lightning Bolt, Falcon Strike, Sharp Shooting…) hit your opponent, and area skills (Whirlwind, Frost Nova, Arrow Rain, Brandish, Meteor Storm, Storm Gust) catch them if they're close. Freezes and snares don't work on players. Evade cards help against player attacks as well.

### The Arena and the ranking

The Arena is the ring of sand behind the café. **A duel counts for the ranking when both players stand inside the ring when it starts and both are signed in.** The challenge pop-up says whether a duel is ranked.

- Everyone starts with a **rating of 1,000**. The winner takes points from the loser: **16** between equal ratings, more for beating someone rated higher, fewer for beating someone rated lower.
- **Stepping out of the ring during a ranked duel loses it.**
- Every ranked win is announced to the whole town, with the winner's new rating.
- **Magnus the Arena Master** explains the rules and shows your place, rating and wins and losses, plus the ten best players.
- Guests can duel anywhere, but only signed-in players are ranked.

## 11. Open PVP: the Wildlands

North of town, past a dashed red line with little red flags, lie **the Wildlands** (levels 30–40). There's no law up there: **players can attack each other without asking.** Two paths run north along the town walls, one from the west road and one from the east road, with a sign where each crosses the line. **Sable the Bounty Hunter** waits by the west path to explain the rules, with her **WANTED** board across the path. The minimap shows the Wildlands in red, and a banner tells you when you cross in or out.

### The rules

| Rule | |
| --- | --- |
| Where | Only inside the Wildlands. Step back over the line and nobody can touch you. The town and the other fields stay safe |
| Who | Players from **level 20**. Below that, you can't attack anyone and nobody can attack you |
| How | Click (or tap) a player, then **Attack**. An Outlaw is attacked with a single click, like a monster |
| Damage | Player hits do **half damage**, as in duels. Potions and healing work as usual |
| Skills | Aimed skills hit the player you aim at. Area skills only catch the player you're fighting, never bystanders. Freezes and snares don't work on players |
| Duelists | Two players in a duel only fight each other; nobody else can join in |

### Outlaws and bounties

- **Attack a player who isn't an Outlaw and you become one**, for **3 minutes** from your latest such attack. Your name turns red with a skull (☠), and a timer shows under your gold.
- **Hitting an Outlaw is always free.** So fighting back when someone attacks you never makes you an Outlaw: your attacker already is one.
- **Beaten by a player while you're not an Outlaw, you lose nothing.** You wake up in town as after any fainting, with all your EXP and gold.
- **Beaten while you are an Outlaw,** you lose EXP like any fainting (3% of a level), and a **bounty of 5% of your gold** (at most 5,000) goes to whoever beat you. Then your name is clean again.
- **Fainting to a monster** works as usual, wherever you are.
- **Leaving the game mid-fight** (within 10 seconds of another player's attack) counts as being beaten by them. Leaving and coming back doesn't clear an Outlaw's name either.

### Sable's board

Talk to **Sable** (press E by her) to read her board. It has three pages:

- **Most wanted:** everyone who's an Outlaw right now, the biggest bounty first, with their level, their bounty and how long until their name is clean again. Your own name is marked if you're on it.
- **This week:** the ten best hunters this week, by players beaten in the Wildlands. Level on that, whoever beat more Outlaws goes first. The weekly board starts again every **Monday at midnight**, Batam time.
- **All time:** the same, since the board began.

Both rankings show **your own place** too, with how many players and Outlaws you've beaten and the bounty gold you've collected.

- **Only signed-in players are ranked.** Guests can read the board, but their wins don't go on it.
- A win only counts when it would count for the titles: beating the same player again within 10 minutes doesn't count again.
- When someone takes the lead for the week (with at least 3 players beaten), the whole town hears about it.

### Wildlands titles

Beating players counts towards the Wildlands titles (see Titles): **Raider** (10 players), **Bounty Hunter** (25 Outlaws) and **Reaper** (250 players). Beating the same player again only counts once every 10 minutes, so nobody can farm a friend.

## 12. Titles

Titles are earned by doing things in the game. Open your titles with **T** (or the medal button) to see every title, the ones you've earned, and how far along you are with the rest. Pick one to **show under your name**, in its rarity colour, for everyone to see, or hide it again.

- When you earn a title, a banner pops up over your head and you get a chat line. **Epic and legendary titles are announced to the whole town.**
- A badge on the medal button counts new titles you haven't looked at yet.
- "Help defeat" means you hurt the monster before it fell: everyone in a fight gets the count, not just the one who landed the last hit.
- Cards and legendary items only count when they're **fresh from a monster**, not when someone hands them to you.
- Characters from before titles existed get their level titles straight away when they come back.
- Players beaten in the Wildlands count once every 10 minutes for the same player.

## 14. A good path for new players

1. **Level 1–4:** Slimes by the road, south of town. Buy a few Red Potions and a Dagger or Short Sword from Bram.
2. **Level 4–7:** Grass Snakes in the west grass. Wear what you find.
3. **Level 7–10:** Hornets in the east field. They attack on sight, so keep potions ready (Q).
4. **Level 10:** Visit **Orin** just inside the south gate and choose your job. Spend your first skill point (K).
5. **Level 10–14:** Snapper Flowers by the lake, or Grubs and Night Bats out of the **east gate**.
6. **Level 14–20:** Rock Worms, Gazers and Ghosts deeper in Dusk Hollow. Use skills on Ghosts, since plain attacks often miss them.
7. **Level 20:** Team up for the **Pumpking**. Save your Mithril and Star Crystals for **Hilda**, and aim for your first aura at +7. Ten Pumpking fights make you a **Kingslayer**.
8. **Level 20–25:** Out of the **west gate** into the **Orc Woods**: Goblins in the grass north of the road, then Orc Warriors on the rocks south of it. Knights, try a **Halberd** from level 25.
9. **Level 25–30:** Orc Archers up north, Orc Skeletons west of the inlet, and Orc Zombies far to the south. At 30, gather a party for the **Orc Warlord**.
10. **Level 30–35:** Take the path north from the west road, past **Sable**, into the **Wildlands**. Read her rules first: other players can attack you up there. Boarmen roam past the west path; Lizardmen past the east path only fight back. Bram's **White Potions** heal 250 HP.
11. **Level 35–40:** Werewolves in the north-west woods, Trolls on the highlands, Vampires on the high ground to the north-east. From 40, bring a party for the **Minotaur King**, and watch your back.
12. **Level 40–45:** Take the path east off the south road to **Edda**'s pier and sail to **Frostfell** (500 gold). Yetis jump you in the woods west of the pier; Ramhorns to the east only fight back. Then the Frost Shamans on the west shore: close in fast, they throw ice.
13. **Level 45–50:** Frost Wights on the icy highlands and Frost Drakes in the east. At 50, bring a party of four for the **Frost Giant** at the end of the path. **Bjorn** sails you home for free.
14. **Level 50:** Back to **Orin** for your second job: **Lord Knight**, **Wizard** or **Hunter**. Spend your new points on the skills on keys 4–6.
15. **Level 50–55:** Down the east road past the Pumpking to **Finn**'s boat, and over to **Ravenmoor** (800 gold). Wraiths in the woods by the pier dodge plain attacks: use your skills. Then the Gargoyles either side of the castle.
16. **Level 55–60:** Dark Knights below the castle gate, Liches in the east, Flesh Golems in the south. At 60, bring a party of four into the castle for the **Fallen King**. **Corvin** rows you home for free.

Along the way, open your titles (T) now and then: you'll have earned a few.

Sell your monster loot to Bram whenever your bag fills up, and sit down (X) to heal faster between fights. Spare gear, ingots and cards are worth more to other players: open a shop in the Market Square, or look in theirs for what you need.

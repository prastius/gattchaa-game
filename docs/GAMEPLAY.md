# Gattchaa Town: Gameplay Guide

Gattchaa Town is a small online world for card collectors, built around the Gattchaa Oripa shop. You walk around a walled royal town with other players, chat, open your own shop, and head out of the gates to fight monsters, level up and collect gear, much like Ragnarok Online or TibiaME.

The game has three parts:

1. **Hanging out.** The town is a meeting place. Chat, sit by the fountain, found or join a guild, visit the Gattchaa staff, and run your own shop in the Market Square: trade gear, ingots and monster cards for gold, and show off your real cards.
2. **Adventure.** Outside the walls, monsters wait in fields sorted by level. Level up, choose a job, find gear and monster cards, and upgrade your equipment.
3. **Player vs player.** Challenge anyone to a duel, climb the ranking in the Arena, fight anyone (and be fought) in the Wildlands, the open PVP field north of town, or fight with your guild for Crownhold, the guild castle, twice a week.

> **One rule about money.** Real cards are bought and paid for only on gattchaaoripa.com. In-game gold buys in-game things (potions, gear, upgrades, other players' loot) and never real cards.

## 1. Getting started

- **Play as a guest** with just a name, or **Sign in with Gattchaa** using your gattchaaoripa.com account. The sign-in happens on the Gattchaa site, so the game never sees your password.
- **Guests** can do everything except open a shop (they can still buy from one), but they start over on their next visit.
- **Signed-in players** keep their character, level, bag, gear and skills on any device. Only one window per account is allowed: opening a second one takes over from the first.
- **Your look.** Choose a body, hair, clothes and colours when you join. You can change them any time with the **Wardrobe** button. Gear you wear (helmets, armour, weapons…) shows on top of your look.
- New players appear on the main avenue, between the fountain plaza and the Market Square.

## 3. The town

Gattchaa Town is a walled royal town in the middle of an island, with gates to the west, east and south.

- **Gattchaa Palace** (north) is the Gattchaa store. Oripa machines stand at its doors, one per franchise. Inside are a machine gallery, the Battle Hall, and counters for pickups and the newest packs.
- **The palace forecourt** has the Ichiban Kuji house and the Card Grading house.
- **The fountain plaza** is where the main street crosses the grand avenue. Wandering townsfolk here share tips.
- **The Rooms pavilion** (west of the plaza) has tables showing live Sharing Pack and Packs Battle rooms.
- **The pack stalls** (east of the plaza) are four counters run by Gattchaa staff.
- **The Market Square** (by the south gate) is where players run their own shops.
- **The lakeside park** runs along the west wall, with a pond, benches and a campfire.
- **The Collectors' Café** is in the east quarter, with **the Arena** behind it: a ring of sand where players duel, with benches for the crowd.
- **The gates** lead to the monster fields: south to the South Meadow, east to Dusk Hollow, west to the Orc Woods. From the west and east roads, paths run north along the town walls to the Wildlands, and beyond them a long bridge crosses the sea to Crownhold, the guild castle. Off the south road, a path runs east to the meadow's south-east point, where a ferry sails to the snowy island of Frostfell, and on east to the meadow's east shore, where a ship sails to the spring island of Petalfall (and three more from the same pier, to the islands far out in the eastern sea: Ashpeak, Sunscar and Starfall). And the east road runs on past the Pumpking to a boat for the autumn island of Ravenmoor.

The town is always safe: monsters never come in, and nobody can attack anyone inside the walls, except in a duel both players agreed to.

**Day and night** follow Batam time. At night the lamps, windows, campfire and shop signs glow and fireflies come out. By day, butterflies fly over the flowers. When someone pulls a big hit on Gattchaa, the whole town hears about it.

## 4. Player shops

Ragnarok-style vending. Signed-in players sit down in the Market Square and sell two kinds of things:

- **Things from their bag, for in-game gold:** gear (upgrades and cards stay in it), monster cards, ingots, potions, loot.
- **Their real cards,** the ones they've listed on the Gattchaa marketplace. These are only ever bought on gattchaaoripa.com, never with gold.

### Opening your shop

1. Walk into the **Market Square** and press **Open shop** (V).
2. Give your shop a name (or leave it empty for "your name's shop").
3. Under **Sell from your bag**, tap **Sell** next to up to **12** things. Set a price in gold for each, and for stacks how many to sell. If you ask less than Bram the Outfitter would pay, the form warns you.
4. The form tells you if your spot is good: not in front of staff, and not so close to another shop that the signs overlap.
5. Press **Open shop here**. Your character sits down on a rug with a sign over your head. The sign shows a coin when you sell only for gold, a card when you also have Gattchaa cards, and how many things are for sale.

You need at least one thing from your bag or one card listed on Gattchaa. Gear you're wearing isn't offered: take it off first.

### While it's open

- What you put in the shop **leaves your bag** until it sells.
- When something sells, **the gold goes straight into your purse** and the chat tells you who bought what. This works **even while you're away**: when you leave the game, a look-alike minds your shop for **24 hours**, and the next time you come back, the chat tells you what sold.
- To change prices or what's for sale, close the shop and open it again.
- **Closing:** press **Close shop**. Whatever didn't sell comes back to your bag. If your bag is full, it waits for you and arrives as soon as you make room.
- **Sold out:** when everything has sold (and you have no Gattchaa cards to show), the shop closes by itself.

### Buying

- Click a shop's sign (or browse it from Tina's list) to see what it sells, with prices in gold.
- Press **Buy** for one, or **All** for the whole stack. You must be in the Market Square, have the gold and have room in your bag.
- Upgraded gear and gear with cards keep them when you buy them.
- Real cards in the shop link to their listing on gattchaaoripa.com, where you buy and pay as usual.

### Finding things

Ask **Tina** in the Market Square:

- **Shops** lists every open shop, who runs it, whether they're in town, and what it sells. Tap one to browse it.
- **Find an item** lists everything for sale for gold in every shop, cheapest first. Type a name ("mithril", "saber", "slime card") to narrow it down, then **Visit** the shop.

Shops also show as orange dots on the minimap.

### How fighting works

- **Click a monster** (or press Space) and your character walks up and attacks it about once a second, as long as it's in reach. Bows and staves attack from a distance.
- **Missing.** You land 95% of your hits against monsters at or below your level. Each level the monster is above you takes 4% off, down to 60%.
- **Critical hits.** 5% of hits are critical: they ignore defence and do 1.5× damage. Some cards and skills raise this chance.
- **Defence** softens hits but never blocks them completely. Defence equal to the attack halves a hit.
- **Skills** always hit and never miss.

### Rewards

- **EXP** is shared by everyone who hurt the monster, by how much damage each person did. **In a party**, what the party earned is shared evenly by every member close by, with a bonus (see "Parties").
- **Gold** goes to whoever did the most damage.
- **Easy monsters give less.** A monster more than 5 levels below you gives 15% less EXP for each extra level, down to 10%.
- **Level-ups** heal you fully. Every 10th level is announced in chat.
- The level cap is **100**.

### Healing and fainting

- Out of combat you slowly get HP and MP back: a full heal takes about a minute.
- **Sit down** (X) to heal 3× faster. Resting **inside the town walls** doubles that again.
- **Potions** heal straight away, even in the middle of a fight: one every 0.8 seconds. Q drinks the smallest potion that heals you fully.
- From **level 70** you can drink **Golden Potions** (600 HP, from Bram). Below that they're too strong for you, and Q skips them.
- **Fainting.** At 0 HP you faint and wake up back in town, fully healed. Novices lose nothing. Knights, Mages and Archers lose 3% of a level's EXP.
- **Beaten by another player** in the Wildlands, you lose nothing, unless you're an Outlaw (see "Open PVP: the Wildlands").

### Field bosses

**How every field boss fights.** Besides its plain attacks, every field boss has two area attacks, and a **red circle** on the ground warns you about a second before each one lands:

- **Slam:** a circle round the boss itself, when someone's close to it. Everyone still inside when it lands takes a heavy hit. Fighters up close: step out, then step back in.
- **Blast:** a circle under whoever is fighting it from **furthest away**. Archers and mages: keep moving.
- **Long reach:** a boss's plain attack reaches as far as the longest bow (the Orc Warlord and the Minotaur King still swing up close), so there's nowhere to stand and shoot it for free, and a boss you're shooting doesn't wander home and leave you be.

| Boss | Slam (circle round it) | Blast (circle under the furthest attacker) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pumpking | 1.4× its ATK, every 10 s | 1.2× its ATK, every 12 s |
| Orc Warlord, Minotaur King | 1.5×, every 9 s | 1.3×, every 11 s |
| Frost Giant, Fallen King | 1.6×, every 9 s | 1.4×, every 11 s |
| Ogre Warlord | 1.6×, every 8.5 s | 1.4×, every 10.5 s |
| Cinder Colossus | 1.7×, every 8.5 s | 1.5×, every 10.5 s |
| Dune King | 1.7×, every 8 s | 1.5×, every 10 s |
| Star Tyrant | 1.8×, every 8 s | 1.6×, every 10 s |

A party that steps out of the circles takes far less damage than one that stands still. Even a level-74 Hunter shooting the Ogre Warlord from the edge of its reach takes a beating now (and dodging the blasts cuts that by most of the area damage).

**The Pumpking** lives where the east road ends in Dusk Hollow. It's a Ragnarok-style MVP:

- There's only one. It has 12,000 HP, breathes fire from a distance, and heals slowly if left alone.
- It's drawn big, with its name and health bar always showing.
- When it falls, the whole town hears who brought it down. It comes back **10 minutes** later, and the town hears that too.
- A level 20 Knight can just about beat it alone with plenty of potions. A party of three takes about 80 seconds.
- **It always drops** King's Candy (sells for 600 gold), a Mithril Ingot and usually an Orange Potion. It often drops good gear (Crusader Shield, Greathelm, Great Bow, Ember Staff, Knight's Blade), and half the time a Star Crystal. Rarely it drops the **Moonblade** (8%) or the legendary **Pumpking Card** (2%).

Everyone starts as a **Novice**. At **level 10**, visit **Orin the Job Master** just inside the south gate and choose a job. **A job is for life**: you can't change it later.

| Job | Strengths | Weapons | Lv 20 HP / MP / ATK / DEF | Starting weapon |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Novice | (until level 10) | Swords, daggers | Lv 10: 148 / 37 / 24 / 5 | |

These numbers are without any gear. Your stats grow automatically every level; there are no stat points to spend.

### Second jobs

A second job has to be earned: pass the **Trial of Mastery** (below) and **Orin** gives it to you, from **level 50**. Each job has one, and it's for life too:

| First job | Second job | What changes | Lv 60 HP / MP / ATK / DEF |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Knight | **Lord Knight** | Pierces armour, sweeps crowds aside, and fights harder the longer it lasts. +69 HP, +10 ATK at the change | 1,083 / 208 / 154 / 50 |
| Mage | **Wizard** | Commands the sky: meteors, blizzards and lightning on whole crowds. +35 HP, +12 ATK at the change | 606 / 547 / 168 / 20 |
| Archer | **Hunter** | A falcon on the arm and snares in the pack: pins the prey down, then never misses. +45 HP, +10 ATK at the change | 746 / 281 / 153 / 30 |

- **You keep everything:** your level, EXP, gear, skills and skill points. A Lord Knight still wears Knight gear, a Wizard staves, a Hunter bows.
- **Three new skills** join your first three, on keys **4, 5 and 6**.
- Your stats grow a little faster from then on, so the change itself is a step up (you're fully healed too).
- There's no gift weapon this time, and the whole town hears about it.

| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Lord Knight | **Paladin** | Sworn to the light: holy fire from the sky, a divine shield, and judgement on everything around it. +99 HP, +14 ATK at the change | 1,702 / 328 / 243 / 80 |

- **You keep everything** again: all six skills so far, your gear and your points. A Paladin still wears Knight gear.
- **Three more skills**, on keys **7, 8 and 9**. With nine skills, the 91 points you have at level 100 are enough to max nearly all of them (90 points max all nine).

### The Trial of Mastery

Ragnarok style, the way to your second job is a quest in three steps, and the way to your third job is the same quest again. Orin's window shows the steps, ticked off as you go.

1. **Proof from Frostfell.** From **level 45**, talk to **Orin** and press **Set me the trial**. He asks for loot from Frostfell's monsters, different for each job. They drop it often, or you can buy it in other players' shops. When you have it all, press **Hand over the proof**: he takes all of it at once.

   | Job | Proof |
   | --- | --- |
   | Knight | 20 Wight's Chain (Frost Wights) and 20 Drake Scale (Frost Drakes) |
   | Mage | 20 Frozen Tear (Frost Shamans) and 20 Drake Scale (Frost Drakes) |
   | Archer | 20 Ram Horn (Ramhorns) and 20 Yeti Fur (Yetis) |

2. **The Shade.** From **level 50**, Orin sends you to the **Isle of Trials**, a small island far out in the western sea (no boat goes there). **Vesna the Trial Keeper** waits at the top of the stone circle. When you're ready, press **Raise the Shade**: a Shade of the job you want rises in the circle, and you have **3 minutes** to beat it. The time left shows at the top of the screen.

   | Your job | Your Shade | Level | HP | How it fights |
   | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
   | Knight | Shade of a Lord Knight | 52 | 22,000 | Up close, and hits hard |
   | Mage | Shade of a Wizard | 52 | 32,000 | Throws fire from afar |
   | Archer | Shade of a Hunter | 52 | 22,000 | Shoots from far away, and fast |

   - **It fights only you.** Nobody else can hit it, and it never goes for anyone else, so other players on the island can't help or get in the way. Each has their own Shade.
   - **Potions are allowed,** and so are your skills. It never heals.
   - **Falling costs nothing here:** no EXP lost. You wake up in town as usual. Running out of time, or pressing **Give up**, doesn't cost anything either. The proof stays handed over: ask Orin to send you back and try again.
   - **Vesna** can send you home at any time (**Take me back to town**).

3. **Your second job.** Beat the Shade and you're taken back to Orin a few seconds later. Talk to him and press **Become…**.

In Frostfell gear with all three first-job skills learned, a level-50 player beats their Shade in a minute or two, with some potions (Knights drink the most; Mages can mostly heal themselves).

**For a third job**, it's the same three steps, from **level 75** (the proof) and **level 80** (the Shade). The proof comes from **Ashpeak**, and the Shades are much stronger:

| Your job | Proof | Your Shade | Level | HP | How it fights |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Lord Knight | 20 Obsidian Shard (Obsidian Knights) and 20 Magma Core (Magma Golems) | Shade of a Paladin | 82 | 58,000 | Up close, and hits very hard |
| Wizard | 20 Salamander Scale (Salamanders) and 20 Magma Core (Magma Golems) | Shade of an Archmage | 82 | 85,000 | Casts from afar, and hits very hard |
| Hunter | 20 Imp Horn (Ember Imps) and 20 Cinder Veil (Cinder Wraiths) | Shade of a Ranger | 82 | 62,000 | Shoots from far away, fast and hard |

In Ashpeak gear with all six skills learned and Golden Potions, a level-80 player beats it in about a minute and a half to two minutes.

### Skill points

- You get **1 skill point** at the job change, and **1 more every level** after that: 41 at level 50, 51 at level 60, 61 at level 70, 71 at level 80, 91 at level 100. Until the late 60s that's not enough to max all six skills, so choose; by level 69 you can max them all. Third jobs have nine skills: 90 points max them all, and you have 91 at level 100.
- Spend them in the skills window (**K**). Each skill goes up to **level 10** and gets stronger every level.
- Use skills with keys **1–9** or the skill buttons. Each skill costs MP and has a cooldown, shown as a shadow on its button. There's also a short pause (0.7 s) between any two skills.
- Aimed skills use your target. Without one, they pick the nearest monster and walk you into range.
- Attack skills don't work inside the town.

### Your bag and gear

- **Eight equipment slots:** weapon, shield, head, armour, legs, feet, ring and amulet. The first six show on your character for everyone to see.
- **The bag** holds 30 stacks. Potions, loot, ingots and cards stack up to 99; equipment doesn't stack.
- Click an item in the bag to see it. Double-click to wear it or drink it. You can drop things on the ground for anyone to pick up.
- Some gear needs a minimum level, and some is only for certain jobs.

## 9. The Blacksmith: upgrades and auras

**Hilda the Blacksmith** works by her forge fire outside the south gate. She makes your gear stronger, from **+0 to +10**.

### How upgrading works

- Every try costs **gold** and **one ingot**.
- **+1 to +4 always work.** After that, a try can fail.
- **A failed try drops the gear one level** (+6 fails and becomes +4). **It never breaks**, and you never lose the item.
- Upgrading works on weapons, shields, helmets, armour, legs and boots. **Rings and amulets can't be upgraded.**

| +2 | 100% | 200 | Iron Ingot |
| +3 | 100% | 300 | Iron Ingot |

### What each level adds

- **Weapons:** more ATK every level, about an eighth of the weapon's own ATK (at least +1). For example, a Saber gets ATK +2 per level, a Knight's Blade +3 and a Moonblade +4. A +10 Moonblade has ATK +72 instead of +32.
- **Everything else:** DEF +1 per level.

## 10. Duels and the Arena

Fight other players, just for fun or for the ranking. **Nobody ever loses EXP, gold or items in a duel**, and both players are healed when it ends.

### How a duel works

1. **Challenge.** Click (or tap) another player standing near you and press **Challenge to a duel**. They get a pop-up and have 20 seconds to **Accept** or **Decline**.
2. **Countdown.** "3, 2, 1, FIGHT!" appears over both of you. You automatically aim at your opponent.
3. **Fight.** Attack like you would a monster: click them or press Space, and use your skills (keys 1–9). A bar across the top shows who you're fighting and the time left, with a **Give up** button.
4. **The end.** Whoever drops to 0 HP loses. The winner gets a "WINNER!" banner, and you're both healed straight away.

### Rules

| Rule | Why |
| --- | --- |
| Player hits do **half damage** | So a duel lasts long enough for skills and tactics to matter |
| **No potions** and no healing over time | Otherwise nobody could ever win. A Mage's Heal skill still works |
| **2 minutes** at most | After that it's a draw |
| Stay close: walking too far from your opponent calls it off | It's a draw |
| Giving up, leaving the game, or fainting to a monster | The other player wins |
| Duels work anywhere, even inside the town | Only the two duelists can hurt each other; nobody else can join in |
| You can't duel while minding your shop | Close the shop first |

Skills work on players too: aimed skills (Bash, Double Shot, Fire Bolt, Spiral Pierce, Lightning Bolt, Falcon Strike, Sharp Shooting…) hit your opponent, and area skills (Whirlwind, Frost Nova, Arrow Rain, Brandish, Meteor Storm, Storm Gust) catch them if they're close. Freezes and snares don't work on players. Evade cards help against player attacks as well.

### The Arena and the ranking

The Arena is the ring of sand behind the café. **A duel counts for the ranking when both players stand inside the ring when it starts and both are signed in.** The challenge pop-up says whether a duel is ranked.

- Everyone starts with a **rating of 1,000**. The winner takes points from the loser: **16** between equal ratings, more for beating someone rated higher, fewer for beating someone rated lower.
- **Stepping out of the ring during a ranked duel loses it.**
- Every ranked win is announced to the whole town, with the winner's new rating.
- **Magnus the Arena Master** explains the rules and shows your place, rating and wins and losses, plus the ten best players.
- Guests can duel anywhere, but only signed-in players are ranked.

## 11. Open PVP: the Wildlands

North of town, past a dashed red line with little red flags, lie **the Wildlands** (levels 30–40). There's no law up there: **players can attack each other without asking.** Two paths run north along the town walls, one from the west road and one from the east road, with a sign where each crosses the line. **Sable the Bounty Hunter** waits by the west path to explain the rules, with her **WANTED** board across the path. The minimap shows the Wildlands in red, and a banner tells you when you cross in or out.

### The rules

| Rule | |
| --- | --- |
| Where | Only inside the Wildlands. Step back over the line and nobody can touch you. The town and the other fields stay safe |
| Who | Players from **level 20**. Below that, you can't attack anyone and nobody can attack you |
| How | Click (or tap) a player, then **Attack**. An Outlaw is attacked with a single click, like a monster |
| Damage | Player hits do **half damage**, as in duels. Potions and healing work as usual |
| Skills | Aimed skills hit the player you aim at. Area skills only catch the player you're fighting, never bystanders. Freezes and snares don't work on players |
| Duelists | Two players in a duel only fight each other; nobody else can join in |
| Parties | Members of the same party can't attack each other (a duel between them is still fine) |
| Guilds | Members of the same guild can't attack each other either |
| Crownhold | During a siege, inside the castle's walls, guild members fight every other guild's, at any level, without becoming Outlaws (see "Guild wars") |

### Outlaws and bounties

- **Attack a player who isn't an Outlaw and you become one**, for **3 minutes** from your latest such attack. Your name turns red with a skull (☠), and a timer shows under your gold.
- **Hitting an Outlaw is always free.** So fighting back when someone attacks you never makes you an Outlaw: your attacker already is one.
- **Beaten by a player while you're not an Outlaw, you lose nothing.** You wake up in town as after any fainting, with all your EXP and gold.
- **Beaten while you are an Outlaw,** you lose EXP like any fainting (3% of a level), and a **bounty of 5% of your gold** (at most 5,000) goes to whoever beat you. Then your name is clean again.
- **Fainting to a monster** works as usual, wherever you are.
- **Leaving the game mid-fight** (within 10 seconds of another player's attack) counts as being beaten by them. Leaving and coming back doesn't clear an Outlaw's name either.

### Sable's board

Talk to **Sable** (press E by her) to read her board. It has three pages:

- **Most wanted:** everyone who's an Outlaw right now, the biggest bounty first, with their level, their bounty and how long until their name is clean again. Your own name is marked if you're on it.
- **This week:** the ten best hunters this week, by players beaten in the Wildlands. Level on that, whoever beat more Outlaws goes first. The weekly board starts again every **Monday at midnight**, Batam time.
- **All time:** the same, since the board began.

Both rankings show **your own place** too, with how many players and Outlaws you've beaten and the bounty gold you've collected.

- **Only signed-in players are ranked.** Guests can read the board, but their wins don't go on it.
- A win only counts when it would count for the titles: beating the same player again within 10 minutes doesn't count again.
- When someone takes the lead for the week (with at least 3 players beaten), the whole town hears about it.

### Wildlands titles

Beating players counts towards the Wildlands titles (see Titles): **Raider** (10 players), **Bounty Hunter** (25 Outlaws) and **Reaper** (250 players). Beating the same player again only counts once every 10 minutes, so nobody can farm a friend.

## 12. Parties

Hunt together, Ragnarok style. **Click (or tap) another player** and press **Invite to party**. They get a pop-up and have 30 seconds to press **Join**. Whoever invites first becomes the party's **leader** (♛).

- **Up to 6** in a party. Only the leader invites new members, and can remove anyone with the × by their name.
- **Your party** shows under your status (on phones, at the bottom left, above the skill bar): everyone's name, level, job and health, wherever they are. Party members' names show in **green** above their heads, and they're **green dots** on your minimap.
- **Party chat:** type **/p** and a message (for example `/p meet at the west gate`). Only your party sees it, in green.
- **Leaving:** press **Leave** on the party panel. Leaving the game leaves the party too. If the leader leaves, the next member becomes the leader; a party of one breaks up.

### Sharing EXP

When a monster falls, the EXP your party earned from it (everyone's damage added up) is **shared evenly** by every member who's alive and close by, about 30 tiles, **even the ones who never hit it**. So whoever takes the hits, or stands back and heals, levels up as fast as whoever lands the blows.

- **A bonus for each member:** every member sharing a kill after the first adds **10%**. Two of you share 110% of the monster's EXP, three share 120%, a full party of six shares 150%.
- Each member's share then gets their own level penalty, as usual (monsters far below your level give less).
- **Everyone sharing must be within 15 levels** of each other. If the party's spread is wider, that kill goes by damage, just as if you weren't in a party.
- Every member who shared a kill counts it for their titles ("help defeat").

### Loot and fighting

- Loot from a monster your party fought is yours to pick up for the first 15 seconds, whoever in the party did the most damage.
- Gold still goes to whoever did the most damage.
- In the Wildlands, members of the same party **can't attack each other**.

## 13. Guilds

A party is for one hunt; a **guild** is for good. Ragnarok style, it has a name and an emblem that every member wears over their head, a leader and officers, a notice for everyone, a purse, and its own chat. It stays when you leave the game, and it's still there tomorrow.

- **Guilds are for signed-in players.** A guild belongs to its members' accounts, so guests can't found or join one.
- **Up to 30** members.

### Founding one

Talk to **Leopold the Guild Registrar**, on the main street just east of the fountain plaza (there's a GUILDS signpost). You need **level 20** and **10,000 gold**.

- **Pick a name:** 3 to 16 letters or digits (spaces, ' and - are fine too, but not only digits). Every guild's name is its own: nobody else can take it, whatever the capitals.
- **Pick an emblem:** one of 12 symbols (★ ✦ ♛ ♞ ♠ ♣ ♥ ♦ ✿ ☾ ❖ ✠) in one of 9 colours. The form shows how it will look.
- Press **Found the guild**. You lead it, and the whole town hears about it.

### Ranks

| Rank | What they can do |
| --- | --- |
| Leader (♛) | Everything: invite, remove anyone, make members officers (or members again), change the notice, and hand the guild to someone else |
| Officer (✦) | Invite players, remove members (not other officers or the leader), and change the notice |
| Member | Talk on the guild chat, give gold to the purse, and leave whenever they like |

### Inviting

**Click (or tap) a player** and press **Invite to guild** (leaders and officers only). They get a pop-up and have 30 seconds to press **Join**. Players in another guild, and guests, can't be invited.

### The guild window (G)

Press **G** (or the shield button) to open it:

- Your guild's emblem and name, your rank, and its record in the guild wars: whether it holds Crownhold, sieges won and crystals broken.
- **The notice:** a short message for every member (when you meet, who to ask, what's next). The leader and officers write it; members see it in the window, and in the chat each time they come into town.
- **The purse:** the guild's gold, a box to give some, and the last 10 gifts and hires (see "The guild's purse").
- **Every member:** a green dot when they're online, their level and job, their rank, when they were last seen, and how much gold they've given. The leader and officers have buttons by each name: **Officer** / **Member** (the leader), **Leader** (the leader hands over the guild, and becomes an officer), and **×** to remove someone. Buttons that are hard to undo ask "Sure?" first: click again within 3 seconds.
- **Leave the guild** at the bottom. The leader can't leave while others are in it: make someone else the leader first. The last one out closes the guild, and its name is free again.

### The guild's purse

Every guild has a purse: shared gold that belongs to the guild, not to anyone in it.

- **Any member can give gold**, from anywhere: open the guild window (G), type an amount (**100 gold or more**) and press **Give**, then **Sure?**. Everyone online hears who gave what.
- **It pays for castle guardians.** That's the only thing it buys so far (see "Guardians"). The leader and officers do the hiring, at Aldric.
- **Gold never comes back out.** Nobody can take it out of the purse, not even the leader, so nobody can run off with it. A gift stays in the purse if you leave the guild.
- **Closing the guild loses what's in the purse.** The window warns you first.
- **Holding Crownhold fills it.** Each time a member collects the daily tribute, the castle pays **2,000 gold** more into the purse.
- **The history** shows the last 10 gifts and hires, so everyone can see where the gold went (the castle's payments aren't listed). Hover over a line to see when it happened.

### The top guilds

**Leopold** keeps the ranking of every guild; anyone can read the top ten at his desk, guests too. If your guild isn't in the top ten, its own place is shown underneath.

| Ranked by | |
| --- | --- |
| 1. Castles held | The guild holding Crownhold (🏰) comes first |
| 2. Sieges won | One each time a guild holds the castle as a siege ends |
| 3. Crystals broken | One each time a member lands the last hit on the crystal |
| 4. Levels | All its members' levels added up |
| 5. Age | The older guild |

### Everything else

- **Over your head:** your guild's emblem and name, in its colour, above your name. Click a player to see their guild on their card.
- **Guild chat:** type **/g** and a message (for example `/g guild hunt at eight`). Only your guild sees it, in blue.
- **Online and offline:** when a member comes into town or leaves, the others get a line in the chat.
- **No fighting each other:** in the Wildlands, members of the same guild can't attack each other (a duel is still fine).
- **Guilds and parties are separate:** you can hunt in a party with anyone, in your guild or not.

## 14. Guild wars: Crownhold

Ragnarok's War of Emperium, Gattchaa style. **Crownhold**, the guild castle, stands on its own island in the sea north-east of the Wildlands. Twice a week it's under siege, and the guild holding it when the siege ends keeps it until the next one.

### Getting there

Take the path north from the east road into the **Wildlands** (where other players can attack you), then the road east from the top of the path, north past the Vampires' high ground and east again to the sea. A signpost marks the start of the **long bridge**: about 60 tiles of planks over the sea to Crownhold's island. **Aldric the Castle Herald** waits at the camp outside the castle gate.

### When

Every **Wednesday and Saturday, 8 to 9 pm** Batam time. The whole town hears when a siege begins and ends. While one is on, a bar at the top of the screen shows the time left and who holds the castle. The rest of the week Crownhold is open to everyone, and nobody can fight there.

### The castle

- An **outer wall** with its gate facing west, towards the bridge, and a tower at each corner and either side of the gate.
- Inside, a **keep** with its own walls. Its gate faces **east**, away from the castle gate, so attackers have to go all the way round it.
- In the middle of the keep, behind a red carpet and in front of the lord's seat, stands **the crystal**: 60,000 HP, DEF 50. It never moves or fights back, and it heals slowly if it's left alone.
- The holding guild's colour and emblem fly on every tower (plain grey flags while nobody holds it).

### The rules of a siege

| What | How it works |
| --- | --- |
| Who goes in | Only **guild members** may go inside the walls. Anyone else inside when a siege begins is put outside the gate, and can't walk back in until it's over |
| Fighting | Inside the walls, every guild fights every other: click a member of another guild to attack them. **Any level**, and **nobody becomes an Outlaw**. Party mates and guild mates never fight each other |
| Guardians | The holding guild's hired guards attack any other guild's members inside the walls (see "Guardians") |
| Falling | Costs **nothing**, whether a player or a guardian beat you: no EXP, no gold. Get up, and you're at the camp outside the gate, ready to walk back in |
| The crystal | Only members of a guild that **doesn't** hold the castle can strike it, and only during a siege |
| Breaking it | The guild of whoever lands the **last hit** takes the castle at once, and the whole town hears. Everyone inside who isn't in that guild is thrown out to the camp. The crystal is whole again **10 seconds** later: now the new holders must defend it |
| The end | Whoever holds Crownhold when the hour is up **keeps it** until the next siege. Their members who are in town at that moment become **Castle Lords** |

Three level-70 Lord Knights break the crystal in a little over a minute when nobody stops them, so the holders need to guard the keep's gate, or hire someone to do it.

### Guardians

The **leader and officers** of the guild holding Crownhold can hire guardians from **Aldric**, paid from the **guild's purse** (see "The guild's purse"). There are **eight posts**, each for one guardian: he shows what's in the purse and lists them, with **Hire** buttons by the empty ones.

| Guardian | Cost | HP | ATK | DEF | Fights |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Castle Knight | 5,000 gold | 20,000 | 215 | 80 | Up close |
| Castle Archer | 4,000 gold | 12,000 | 165 | 50 | Shoots arrows from far away |

| Post | Guardians |
| --- | --- |
| Castle gate | 2 Knights and an Archer |
| Keep gate | 2 Knights and an Archer |
| Crystal | A Knight and an Archer |

All eight cost 37,000 gold.

- **Hire them before the siege:** nobody can hire during one.
- **Between sieges** they stand at their posts in plain view. They ignore everyone, and nobody can strike them.
- **During a siege** they attack any other guild's members inside the walls, and chase them a little way from their posts. They never touch their own guild, or anyone outside the walls. Their own guild can't strike them, and area skills pass them by.
- **They give no EXP and no loot.** They're just in the way.
- **A guardian that falls is gone** until someone hires another. Those still standing when the siege ends are healed and stay hired for the next one, even if the server restarts.
- **If the castle changes hands, all of them leave,** and the gold paid for them doesn't come back. The new holders hire their own, once the siege is over.

Guardians won't hold the castle alone, but they buy time. Four level-70 Lord Knights who needed about 80 seconds to reach and break an undefended crystal took about three and a half minutes against all eight, and had to cut down every one. Add a few defenders, and that's a very different fight.

### Holding the castle

- **The tribute.** Every member of the holding guild can collect a tribute from **Aldric** once a day (from midnight Batam time): **3,000 to 6,000 gold, 2 Mithril Ingots, a Star Crystal and 10 White Potions**, sometimes a second Star Crystal (1 in 4), and very rarely a monster card (3%). Keep **4 bag slots** free for it. Each collection also pays **2,000 gold** into your guild's purse.
- **Guardians.** The leader and officers can hire them from Aldric with the purse (see "Guardians"). Seven members collecting every day pay for a full garrison between one siege and the next.
- **The title.** Castle Lord ✦ (epic), with its own aura: a violet ring with a column of light and gold and violet sparks.
- **The banners.** Your guild's emblem over every tower, for everyone crossing the bridge to see.

If the holding guild closes, Crownhold has no lord until the next siege.

## 17. A good path for new players

1. **Level 1–4:** Slimes by the road, south of town. Buy a few Red Potions and a Dagger or Short Sword from Bram.
2. **Level 4–7:** Grass Snakes in the west grass. Wear what you find.
3. **Level 7–10:** Hornets in the east field. They attack on sight, so keep potions ready (Q).
4. **Level 10:** Visit **Orin** just inside the south gate and choose your job. Spend your first skill point (K).
5. **Level 10–14:** Snapper Flowers by the lake, or Grubs and Night Bats out of the **east gate**.
6. **Level 14–20:** Rock Worms, Gazers and Ghosts deeper in Dusk Hollow. Use skills on Ghosts, since plain attacks often miss them.
7. **Level 20:** Team up for the **Pumpking**. Save your Mithril and Star Crystals for **Hilda**, and aim for your first aura at +7. Ten Pumpking fights make you a **Kingslayer**.
8. **Level 20–25:** Out of the **west gate** into the **Orc Woods**: Goblins in the grass north of the road, then Orc Warriors on the rocks south of it. Knights, try a **Halberd** from level 25.
9. **Level 25–30:** Orc Archers up north, Orc Skeletons west of the inlet, and Orc Zombies far to the south. At 30, gather a party for the **Orc Warlord**.
10. **Level 30–35:** Take the path north from the west road, past **Sable**, into the **Wildlands**. Read her rules first: other players can attack you up there. Boarmen roam past the west path; Lizardmen past the east path only fight back. Bram's **White Potions** heal 250 HP.
11. **Level 35–40:** Werewolves in the north-west woods, Trolls on the highlands, Vampires on the high ground to the north-east. From 40, bring a party for the **Minotaur King**, and watch your back.
12. **Level 40–45:** Take the path east off the south road to **Edda**'s pier and sail to **Frostfell** (500 gold). Yetis jump you in the woods west of the pier; Ramhorns to the east only fight back. Then the Frost Shamans on the west shore: close in fast, they throw ice.
13. **Level 45–50:** Frost Wights on the icy highlands and Frost Drakes in the east. At 50, bring a party of four for the **Frost Giant** at the end of the path. **Bjorn** sails you home for free.
14. **Level 45–50:** Ask **Orin** about the **Trial of Mastery** at 45, and keep the Frostfell loot he asks for instead of selling it. At 50 he sends you to the **Isle of Trials**: beat your Shade (bring potions), then come back for your second job: **Lord Knight**, **Wizard** or **Hunter**. Spend your new points on the skills on keys 4–6.
15. **Level 50–55:** Down the east road past the Pumpking to **Finn**'s boat, and over to **Ravenmoor** (800 gold). Wraiths in the woods by the pier dodge plain attacks: use your skills. Then the Gargoyles either side of the castle.
16. **Level 55–60:** Dark Knights below the castle gate, Liches in the east, Flesh Golems in the south. At 60, bring a party of four into the castle for the **Fallen King**. **Corvin** rows you home for free.
17. **Level 60–65:** Take the path east off the south road, straight on past Edda's pier, to **Marlow**'s ship on the meadow's east shore, and sail to **Petalfall** (1,200 gold). Moon Hares by the pier only fight back but dodge plain attacks: use your skills. Then the Fox Spirits in the north-west woods (close in fast, they throw fire) and the Crow Blades in the middle.
18. **Level 65–70:** Phantom Swordsmen in the north-east, Red Ogres in the south (they only fight back, but they hit very hard). At 70, bring a party of four up the road to the **Ogre Warlord**'s camp. **Willow** sails you home for free.
19. **Level 70–75:** Back to the meadow's east shore, where **Dorian** sails from Marlow's pier to **Ashpeak** (1,500 gold). Ember Imps swarm the rock north of the pier; Salamanders spit fire from the south-west slopes (close in fast). Then the Obsidian Knights along the north.
20. **Level 75–80:** Cinder Wraiths in the east dodge plain attacks: use your skills. Magma Golems in the south only fight back, but they hit hardest of all. At 80, bring a party of four up the road to the **Cinder Colossus**'s crater. **Cassia** sails you home for free. At 75, ask **Orin** about your **third job** and keep the Ashpeak loot he asks for; at 80, beat your Shade on the Isle of Trials and become a **Paladin**, **Archmage** or **Ranger**.
21. **Level 80–90:** Switch to Bram's **Golden Potions** (from level 70). **Soren** sails from the same pier to **Sunscar** (1,800 gold). Dune Stalkers in the dunes dodge plain attacks; Dust Djinn throw hot wind from the north-west; then the Tomb Wardens and the Desert Raiders' camp in the east. At 90, bring a party for the **Dune King** in his temple.
22. **Level 90–100:** **Lyra** sails to **Starfall** (2,200 gold), where it's always night. Void Bats swarm the south-west, Astral Seers cast from the north-west, Star Knights guard the north, Night Wisps drift along the east shore, and Crystal Golems in the south-east hit hardest of all. At 100, the **Star Tyrant** waits at his altar south of the lake: take your strongest party.

Along the way, open your titles (T) now and then: you'll have earned a few. And from the Orc Woods on, hunt in a party: the bosses are made for four. From level 20, find a guild to join (or found your own with Leopold), so there's always someone to hunt with, and on Wednesday and Saturday evenings, march with it over the long bridge to fight for Crownhold.

Sell your monster loot to Bram whenever your bag fills up, and sit down (X) to heal faster between fights. Spare gear, ingots and cards are worth more to other players: open a shop in the Market Square, or look in theirs for what you need.
